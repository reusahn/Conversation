# Development evaluation methods — manuscript candidate

2026-10-28. **Development-stage methods text; no participant, LLM, sensor, energy, or treatment-effect results.** This candidate connects the public development task contracts and evaluator to `protocol-v3.md` and `task-evaluation-spec.md`. It neither replaces the active analysis nor freezes the final human study. `confirmatory_allowed=false` remains in force.

## Planned task domain and estimand

The planned experiment will compare three names for one low-reasoning setting—Low, Fast, and Eco—while keeping the high setting and all other interface and service policies common. Its quality outcome will be the final user artifact for each assigned programming task, rather than the first model response, the last request, or the number of passing model calls. Manual editing and completion without an AI call may be permitted under a common policy fixed before the main study. An artifact completed without an AI call can therefore succeed while its first submitted AI mode is `none`.

The present development work uses four public JavaScript task families: summing eligible values, normalizing tags, merging closed intervals, and selecting compatible jobs to maximize reward. Bounded input domains make the development contract's arithmetic, text handling, and output equivalence explicit. They do not establish that tasks are easy for people, sufficient for low reasoning, representative of general AI use, or selected for the final study. The number four is a development inventory, not a frozen final K. Any future participant eligibility based on JavaScript familiarity remains a design candidate requiring independent task and usability validation.

Structural complexity, human-perceived difficulty, low-budget sufficiency, high-budget threshold attainment, and the contrast between budgets are different quantities. In particular, poor low-budget performance alone cannot establish that high reasoning is useful: both budgets may fail. Independent benchmark classification must preserve that possibility. Even classifying a task as below a prespecified success threshold under low and above it under high does not, by itself, establish a separately tested minimum improvement or causal mechanism. A future label-effect interaction must use the classification and uncertainty rule fixed independently of the label-treatment outcomes.

## Development contracts and evaluation

The development contract identifies admissible inputs, the exact output function, output equivalence, and an input-preservation requirement. The public bank and contract carry the identifiers `dev-public-bank-v0.1` and `dev-task-contract-v0.1`, distinct from any future final human or independent benchmark bank. Development inputs and expected results cannot also be described as hidden tests once published. Bounds chosen to avoid floating-point overflow or ambiguous Unicode behavior are engineering restrictions; conclusions about unrestricted numerical inputs, languages, or realistic coding workflows require separate evidence.

For a trusted local candidate function and a valid development bank, the development evaluator makes fresh copies of each input, computes expected results using the task's development oracle, invokes the function, and compares both its output and post-call input with the contract. A task passes only when all required cases have valid judgments and satisfy both output and preservation criteria. An empty bank cannot establish success. Input-preservation checks do not imply that the output itself must be mutable; a readonly array with the correct values is admissible. This trusted-function harness is not a sandbox and must not receive arbitrary participant or externally supplied code. Resource limits, isolation, snapshot loading, and a condition-blind final scoring service remain separate implementation work.

Known faulty solutions and separately constructed counterexamples will be used to challenge the evaluator. Reports must state the actual examined cases, source versions, and execution scope; rejection of those faulty solutions is not evidence that all incorrect programs will be rejected. Development coverage, oracle correctness, and external task validity are different claims. A reference implementation agreeing with an oracle on selected inputs is likewise insufficient to certify the oracle on every admissible input.

## Attribution and missing judgments

The evaluator's judgment must distinguish a valid contract pass, a candidate-attributable contract violation or exception, and a failure of the bank, oracle, evaluator, or result provenance. A candidate wrong output or observed input mutation on an admissible input can be an observed failure. Invalid fixtures, an unknown task contract, a broken expected-result calculation, missing snapshots, or an unreliable scorer cannot be relabeled as participant failure or zero cost. Such cases produce an unknown judgment, preserving the cause and raw record. Candidate exceptions can be attributed only when the invocation occurred on a validated fixture within a healthy trusted harness; arbitrary exceptions crossing an unverified execution boundary do not establish user blame.

In a future main study, scoring will use a final immutable artifact tied to its session, task, snapshot, and contract/scorer/bank versions. Responses arriving after the task or session lock will not replace that artifact. A development function object is not itself proof of such snapshot provenance. Operational tests shown during the task and later research-only scoring must remain separate; their presentation, limits, and costs must be common across label conditions. Condition-blind scoring and a fixed recovery policy will be required before main-study scoring is activated. No such activation is claimed here.

A proposed blind scoring export would contain an arbitrary scoring key, the necessary artifact/snapshot reference, contract and execution versions, and execution/provenance status needed to distinguish candidate and infrastructure failures. Arm names, displayed labels, chosen modes, energy values, and prompt histories would not be provided as scoring predictors. Missing artifacts must remain as unknown entries in the export manifest. A private join would restore every assignment to its original denominator. Removing metadata columns does not certify blinding if artifact contents or filenames reveal the condition; the actual export and scorer access would require inspection. Denominator completeness remains the responsibility of the separate private assignment-manifest join, not of a blind scorer that cannot see the allocation list.

## Link to the planned analysis

All assigned participants and tasks will remain in their planned denominators, including observed failure, abandonment, and genuine non-start. Unknown quality remains distinct from observed failure and will enter the preselected conservative bounds rather than an analysis of complete or successful cases alone. The participant remains the independent analysis unit; development test cases are neither human participants nor independent replications of the label treatment.

Per-arm observed success, observed failure, and unknown counts must accompany the relative quality contrast. A future joint claim requires the preregistered relative quality conditions and a valid reduction in whole-session energy; a passing development artifact cannot establish either claim. The practical noninferiority margins, absolute acceptability policy, final task selection and strata, independent pilot evidence, sample size, and implementation/measurement validity remain unresolved. The present development work does not close those gates or turn the previous score into a passing main-study decision.
